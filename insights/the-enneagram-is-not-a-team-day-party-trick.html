The Enneagram is not a team-day party trick

Used properly, the Enneagram isn't about labelling people. It's about understanding motivation, which is the thing most team interventions never get to.

28 August 2026, 4 min read

Most people meet the Enneagram the same way. Someone sends a free online test, the team compares numbers over lunch, and a few people say "that's so me". By the following week, nothing has changed.

That's a shame, because used properly it's one of the most practical tools I have for working with teams.

It's about motivation, not behaviour

Most personality tools describe what people do. The Enneagram describes why. Two people can behave in exactly the same way, both pushing hard for a deadline, say, for completely different reasons. One needs to be seen as competent. One is anxious about what happens if it goes wrong. Manage them the same way and you'll get it wrong for at least one of them.

When a team understands each other's motivations, a lot of conflict stops being personal. The colleague who keeps asking "what could go wrong?" isn't being negative. They're doing what their type does best, and the team needs it.

How I use it

I use the iEQ9 Professional assessment, not a free quiz, because the depth matters. Everyone gets an individual report and a one-on-one debrief first. Then we bring the team together for a day on working better together: where the friction comes from, what each person needs to do their best work, and what the team as a whole tends to avoid.

The goal is never to put people in boxes. It's the opposite. Once people can see their own patterns, they get a choice about them.

The question isn't "what number are you?" It's "what does the team need from you that your type makes hard to give?"

That's when it stops being a party trick and starts being useful.
