ForContact centres, frontline and service teams where the standards exist but the experience doesn't.

What changesPeople shift how they think about the customer and their own role, so service holds on the difficult calls. Designed to move your CX metrics, not just your training scores.

Case study: customer experience in financial services

+35

point improvement in Net Effort Score

The brief. A major South African financial services group wanted to improve the experience customers had with its service teams. Previous training had covered the standards. The scores hadn't moved.

The approach. Instead of more scripts, we worked on what was driving behaviour: the mental models staff held about customers, the ego states they slipped into under pressure, and who owned the outcome. More than 320 delegates went through the programme.

The result. A 35-point improvement in Net Effort Score, fewer complaints and more compliments. Delegates rated the programme between 4.88 and 4.96 out of 5 across every measure, and 100% said it was worthwhile.

Questions people ask

What's the difference between team coaching and training?

Training builds skills in a group around a defined topic, such as customer experience or leadership. Team coaching works with an intact team on how it works together: its patterns, its relationships and the goal it shares, usually over several sessions. Many of my engagements combine the two.

Why does customer experience training so often fail?

Because it aims at behaviour. Scripts and service standards change what people say for a few weeks, but under pressure people fall back on what they believe about the customer and about themselves. Lasting change comes from working on those mental models. I explain this in Your customer experience training didn't fail. It aimed at the wrong level.

How do you measure whether a programme worked?

We agree the measures at the start, based on your strategy, and report against them at the end. That usually means your own CX or engagement metrics plus delegate feedback. On one financial services programme with more than 320 delegates, Net Effort Score improved by 35 points and delegates rated it between 4.88 and 4.96 out of 5.

Which models and tools do you use?

Transactional Analysis, Functional Fluency, the iEQ9 Enneagram, and the Drama Triangle with its alternative, The Empowerment Dynamic. I use them because they explain what drives behaviour, not to teach frameworks for their own sake.

Which industries do you work in?

Most of my work is in financial services, and increasingly with teams in hospitality, retail, technology and education. Clients have included Henkel, TymeBank, MultiChoice, Momentum, The Capital Hotel Group and AngloGold Ashanti.

Do you work online or in person?

Both. Training, team coaching and individual coaching can run in the room or online. I'm based in Cape Town and work in Johannesburg, across South Africa, and with teams in Europe and Asia.
